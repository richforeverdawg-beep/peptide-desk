# Semaglutide Vial vs Ozempic: Same Molecule, Different Rules

Ozempic is the licensed Novo Nordisk pen. A research vial is lyophilised semaglutide from a synthesis lab. Same peptide sequence. Everything about the product around it differs.

People conflate the two constantly, usually in the direction of assuming a vial is "the same as Ozempic." It's the same molecule and a different product, and the distinction matters more for semaglutide than for anything else on this desk.

## Why semaglutide specifically

Three reasons.

**It's the most recognisable name in the category.** Ozempic became a cultural reference point. That means the highest volume of buyers arriving with a branded expectation, and the highest chance of disappointment when the parcel isn't a pen.

**It's a single agonist.** One receptor. That's the weakest of the GLP-1 options in trial data, and it's the most expensive per milligram here — which is the reverse of what people expect.

**It's the most faked.** Fame plus a cheap-looking vial is a counterfeiter's market.

## Cost per milligram, plainly

Published band on this site is 32 € to 44 € per 5mg vial. That's 6.40 € to 8.80 € per milligram.

Note where that sits. Tirzepatide is 4.80 € to 6.50 € per milligram. Semaglutide is the more expensive molecule per milligram here, despite being the weaker one in trials and the older one on the market.

The reason is the vial size. 5mg against 10mg. Per vial semaglutide looks cheaper; per milligram it isn't.

## Dose and burn rate

A 2.4mg weekly maintenance dose is 9.6mg a month — two vials. A 0.25mg starting dose is 1mg a month, so a single vial lasts five weeks.

That's a wide spread. Semaglutide titration is slow and the maintenance dose is roughly ten times the starting dose. Budget at the dose you're heading for, not the one you're on.

## The titration gap

The licensed schedule steps 0.25 → 0.5 → 1.0 → 2.0 → 2.4mg over months. A vial lets you sit at 0.75mg or 1.25mg if that's what your tolerance wants. Same argument as every vial-versus-pen comparison: range versus convenience.

## Where the comparison breaks

**"It's the same as Ozempic."** Same molecule. No approval behind the vial. If a batch is wrong, there's no regulator to appeal to — only the dealer and the COA.

**"Ozempic is stronger."** Identical sequence. The difference is the product, not the peptide.

**"The pen dose is the dose."** The pen dose is the licensed dose. A vial protocol is a study arm or a convention. Both are reasonable; they're not the same claim.

**"Semaglutide is the budget option."** Cheaper per vial, dearer per milligram, and the weakest trial effect of the GLP-1s on this desk. It's the familiar option, not the cheap one.

## Handling differences that matter

Semaglutide is one of the slower-dissolving peptides. Give it twenty minutes of intermittent swirling and don't force it with extra water — you'll wreck the concentration and make the draw unreadable.

Reconstitute a 5mg vial with 2mL for 2,500 mcg/mL. A 0.25mg dose is then 10 units, and a 2.4mg dose is 96 units — nearly a full barrel. At the top end you're injecting close to a millilitre, which is worth knowing before you buy a box of 0.3mL micro syringes.

## Which to pick

- **Ozempic:** you want the licensed product, a prescription and a dial. Accept the price and the cold chain.
- **Vial:** cost matters, you want titration freedom, or you're already comfortable reconstituting.
