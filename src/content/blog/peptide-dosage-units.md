"Draw 10 units" means nothing on its own. Ten units of a 2,500 mcg/mL solution is 250 mcg. Ten units of a 10,000 mcg/mL solution is 1,000 mcg — four times as much, same barrel mark.

This is the most common error in peptide handling and it is pure arithmetic, so it is entirely fixable.

## The three numbers

Every dose question resolves to three values:

1. **Vial mass** in mg — printed on the label
2. **Water volume** in mL — your choice, and the one variable you control
3. **Target dose** in mcg

Concentration is (1) divided by (2). Units on the barrel is (3) divided by the concentration, then divided by 10.

## Unit conversions you need

- 1 mg = 1,000 mcg
- 1 mL = 100 units on a U-100 insulin syringe
- 1 unit = 0.01 mL

That last one is the whole reason U-100 barrels exist: 100 units to the millilitre, so the barrel doubles as a decimal readout of the volume.

## Worked in full

Target 250 mcg from a 5mg vial.

**Reconstitute with 2mL:**
- 5mg ÷ 2mL = 2.5 mg/mL = 2,500 mcg/mL
- 250 mcg ÷ 2,500 mcg/mL = 0.1 mL
- 0.1 mL × 100 = **10 units**

**Same target, same vial, 1mL of water:**
- 5mg ÷ 1mL = 5,000 mcg/mL
- 250 ÷ 5,000 = 0.05 mL
- **5 units**

Both are 250 mcg. Both are correct. One is twice as easy to read.

| Target dose | From 5mg in 2mL (2,500 mcg/mL) | From 10mg in 2mL (5,000 mcg/mL) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 100 mcg | 4 units | 2 units |
| 250 mcg | 10 units | 5 units |
| 500 mcg | 20 units | 10 units |
| 1,000 mcg | 40 units | 20 units |

Note the right column. A 10mg vial in 2mL is double the concentration, so every draw halves. Same barrel, half the units, same dose. Nothing changed but the arithmetic.

## The dilution trap

More water is not safer. It is easier to read, up to a point.

- **Too little water:** a 5mg vial in 0.5mL is 10,000 mcg/mL. A 250 mcg draw is 2.5 units. On a standard barrel the 2 and the 3 marks are 1mm apart and the graduation lines are 2 units. You cannot hit 2.5 reliably.
- **Too much water:** a 5mg vial in 5mL is 1,000 mcg/mL. A 250 mcg draw is 25 units — perfectly readable — but you're now injecting a quarter of a millilitre per dose, and the vial is punctured more times per unit of peptide.

Sweet spot is a draw between 5 and 30 units. Work backwards from that and pick your water volume, rather than picking a water volume and living with the result.

## Barrels you should not use

**U-40 syringes** are 40 units to the millilitre. Every conversion above is wrong on a U-40 barrel. Check the box.

**Fixed-needle 0.3mL "micro" barrels** are fine and precise, but the graduations are half-units, so a 2.5 unit draw becomes a judgement call. Good for high-concentration, bad for beginners.

**Insulin pens** with cartridges are excellent and completely different maths. If you're on a pen, ignore this article and read the pen's own scale.

## Reading the meniscus

Draw to the mark with the barrel vertical, plunger below, and read at eye level. The top of the liquid curve is the volume, not the edges climbing the glass.

Draw a unit past the mark, then push back to it. It is easier to arrive at a number from above than to creep up on it from below, and it clears air from the needle.

## Tap the bubbles out

Small bubbles in the barrel displace volume. Flick the barrel a few times, then push the plunger until liquid beads at the tip. If you skip this, part of your measured volume is air.

## Write it down

Every vial gets a concentration on the cap and every dose gets a number in a note. Three vials in, "the usual" is a memory exercise, and memory is the weak link.
