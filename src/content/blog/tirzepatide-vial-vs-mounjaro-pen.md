# Tirzepatide Vial vs Mounjaro Pen: What Actually Differs

Tirzepatide is the molecule in both. That's where the similarity ends.

Mounjaro is the licensed Lilly pen, approved for type 2 diabetes in the EU and UK, and sold as Zepbound for weight management. A research vial is lyophilised powder from a synthesis lab with no marketing authorisation behind it. Same active substance, entirely different product.

Both are legitimate. They solve different problems.

## The comparison in one table

| | Mounjaro pen | Research vial |

The gap is large enough that it survives any comparison, and it isn't a mystery. A licensed product carries trial data, a marketing authorisation, a device, pharmacy margin and cold-chain logistics from factory to fridge. A vial carries a synthesis run and a COA.

That's the trade: you are paying the licensed price for the approval, and the vial price for the molecule alone.

## Dosing precision

The pen wins on convenience and loses on range.

Six fixed steps from 2.5 to 15mg, one click each, no arithmetic, no syringe. That's a better experience than reconstituting powder and reading a barrel, and it's the single strongest argument for the pen.

The vial wins on range. A 10mg vial in 2mL is 5,000 mcg/mL, so a 2.5mg dose is 50 units and you can split any increment you want — 1.75mg, 3mg, whatever titration your tolerance actually calls for. With a pen you get the six steps and nothing between them.

For anyone titrating slowly, that's a real difference rather than a preference.

## What the trial data belongs to

Worth being precise: SURMOUNT-1 and SURPASS were run on the licensed product. The 20.9% at 15mg in SURMOUNT-1 ( [NEJM, SURMOUNT-1](https://www.nejm.org/doi/full/10.1056/NEJMoa2206038)) came from a pen under supervision, not from reconstituted vials.

A vial result is a reasonable expectation, not a trial result. Batch purity, your reconstitution accuracy and your storage all sit between the label and the outcome.

## Cold chain

A pen must stay refrigerated from manufacture to use, and a broken chain is invisible from the outside. A lyophilised vial is stable at room temperature for months and only needs the fridge once mixed — meaning transit damage is far less of a risk.

That's the one place the vial is genuinely more robust, and it's usually the reverse of what people assume.

## The prescription question

A pen means a prescriber and a pharmacy. That's a real cost in time and sometimes money, and it's the barrier a vial removes entirely.

What it doesn't remove is the need for judgement. Nobody is checking your titration on a vial.

## Where people go wrong

**"It's the same drug so it's the same thing."** Same molecule, unapproved product. If a dispute reaches a regulator, the vial has no authorisation to appeal to.

**Buying 10mg and treating it like 10mg of pen.** The pen steps and the vial mass are unrelated. 10mg is the vial's total content, not a dose.

**Skipping the COA because the molecule is famous.** Tirzepatide is the most counterfeited peptide there is, precisely because it's the volume leader. Batch ID matching is not optional.

**Reconstituting to a concentration that makes the draw unreadable.** See the arithmetic before the water goes in, not after.

## Which to choose

- **Pen:** you want the licensed product, a fixed schedule and no arithmetic. Accept the price.
- **Vial:** you're cost-sensitive, want titration range, or you're already comfortable with reconstitution. Accept the paperwork burden.
- **Both at once:** entirely reasonable — pen as the base, vial for top-ups between steps.
