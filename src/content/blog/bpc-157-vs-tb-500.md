These two get listed together so often that people assume they're variants of one thing. They aren't. They share a shelf, not a mechanism, and the difference is the reason they're stacked rather than substituted.

Short version, before the detail:

- **BPC-157** is a 15-amino-acid sequence derived from a human gastric juice protein. It survives acid, which is unusual, and the literature is dominated by gut and connective-tissue models.
- **TB-500** is a fragment of thymosin beta-4, an actin-binding protein. It's about cell migration and is studied systemically rather than locally.
- **Neither replaces the other.** They act on different parts of the same repair process, which is why protocols run them together.

## What each actually is

BPC-157 is pentadecapeptide BPC 157, a partial sequence of body protection compound found in human gastric juice. Fifteen residues. It's stable in gastric acid and has an unusually long list of published models behind it, largely from the Sikiric group in Zagreb ( [PubMed, BPC 157 literature](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/?term=BPC+157)).

TB-500 is the synthetic fragment of thymosin beta-4 corresponding to the actin-binding domain, residues 17–23. Thymosin beta-4 is a 43-residue protein; the fragment is what carries the motility activity ( [PubMed, thymosin beta-4 fragment](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/?term=thymosin+beta-4+fragment+TB-500)).

One is a gut-derived pentadecapeptide. The other is a seven-residue piece of a cytoskeletal protein. That's the entire structural story and it explains everything downstream.

## Why they get stacked

Repair has stages, and the two compounds sit in different ones.

**BPC-157** shows angiogenic activity — VEGF receptor signalling, eNOS upregulation — in the models. New blood supply is the first requirement for anything to heal. It also shows up heavily in gut models, which is where the compound came from in the first place.

**TB-500** is about cell migration: actin sequestration, G-actin to F-actin dynamics, endothelial and keratinocyte movement into a wound bed. Building supply lines is one job; getting cells to the site is another.

Angiogenesis then migration. Stacking is a sequence argument, not a synergy claim, and the literature supports them as different axes rather than redundant ones.

## Common research doses

Neither is a licensed medicine, so everything below is a research dose from published work rather than a label:

- **BPC-157:** 200–500 mcg, once or twice daily in most models, often local where the model is local
- **TB-500:** 2–2.5 mg, twice weekly, typically systemic
- **Stacked:** both at the low end of their own range, run concurrently

Note the scale difference. BPC-157 doses in micrograms, TB-500 in milligrams — a thousand-fold gap. Confusing the two is the most common stacking error, and it's a hundred-units-versus-ten-units mistake on the barrel.

Neither has an approved human dose. Anyone quoting a "protocol" is quoting a study arm.

## What the price does not tell you

BPC-157 is the cheapest thing on most dealers' shelves and also the most counterfeited, because it's the one everybody asks for by name. Two vials at 18 € and 30 € can both be BPC-157 and differ entirely in what's in them. The COA is the only separator: lab name, batch ID matching the vial, HPLC purity with the method stated, mass spec confirming identity.

TB-500's fragment structure makes it easier to verify by mass — the theoretical mass is tight and a wrong sequence fails MS. That's a small structural advantage in checking a batch, not a reason to trust a dealer who sends a screenshot instead of a PDF.

## Where people go wrong

**"TB-500 is the stronger BPC-157."** Not stronger, different. If your model is local, TB-500 is a systemic answer to a local question.

**"Run one, then the other."** Sequential works, but you lose the angiogenesis-then-migration sequence that makes stacking coherent. Run them together at the low end.

**"5mg is 5mg, so they cost the same to run."** The vials match; the doses don't. A 5mg TB-500 vial at 2mg twice weekly is one and a bit weeks. A 5mg BPC-157 vial at 250 mcg twice daily is ten days. Different burn rates on identical vials.

**"Both at maximum."** Both at maximum is a lot of volume for one depot. Low end of each, both, is the sane version.
