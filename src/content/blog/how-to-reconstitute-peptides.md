# How to Reconstitute a Peptide Vial Without Ruining It

A lyophilised vial is a cake of powder. Until you add water it is stable for months at room temperature. The moment you add water it becomes a solution with a countdown on it, and every handling error from that point forward is permanent.

Most guides skip the arithmetic and start with "swirl gently." That order is backwards. You need the concentration number before the water goes in, because the number decides how much water.

## Do the maths before you open anything

Concentration is milligrams of peptide divided by millilitres of water. Everything else follows from it.

| Vial | Water added | Concentration | 1 unit on a U-100 barrel |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 5mg | 1mL | 5 mg/mL (5,000 mcg/mL) | 50 mcg |
| 5mg | 2mL | 2.5 mg/mL (2,500 mcg/mL) | 25 mcg |
| 5mg | 2.5mL | 2 mg/mL (2,000 mcg/mL) | 20 mcg |
| 10mg | 2mL | 5 mg/mL (5,000 mcg/mL) | 50 mcg |
| 10mg | 4mL | 2.5 mg/mL (2,500 mcg/mL) | 25 mcg |

A 5mg vial with 2mL of water gives 2,500 mcg/mL. On a U-100 insulin syringe, one unit is 0.01mL, so one unit is 25 mcg. A 250 mcg draw is 10 units. That is a readable, comfortable mark on the barrel.

Aim for a draw between 5 and 30 units. Under 5 units and you're guessing at the meniscus. Over 30 and you're pushing volume into a small depot. If your maths lands outside that window, change the water volume rather than squinting at the barrel.

## What you need

- The lyophilised vial
- Bacteriostatic water — 0.9% benzyl alcohol, preserved, multi-dose. Not sterile water for injection, which has no preservative and gives you a shorter window.
- A 1mL or 3mL syringe with a 25–30G needle for drawing water
- Alcohol swabs
- Insulin syringes for dosing

Bacteriostatic water is the part people skip. Plain sterile water works for a single use, but a reconstituted multi-dose vial needs the benzyl alcohol to hold bacterial growth down between punctures.

## The procedure

1. **Wash your hands.** Boring, and the single biggest contamination source.
2. **Swab both stoppers.** The water vial and the peptide vial. Let them dry — a wet stopper pushes alcohol into the powder.
3. **Draw the water.** Use the volume from your maths, not a rounded guess. Equalise pressure first by injecting an equal volume of air into the water vial.
4. **Angle the needle at the vial wall.** Aim the stream down the glass, not onto the powder cake. A direct jet on the cake can shear the peptide and foam the solution.
5. **Add it slowly.** One steady push. Rushing it is how you get foam and a clumped cake that takes an hour to dissolve.
6. **Swirl, never shake.** Roll the vial between your palms or describe slow circles. Shaking denatures and foams.
7. **Let it sit.** Most vials dissolve in one to five minutes with occasional swirling. Set it down and come back. Do not stab at it.
8. **Check it against the light.** Clear, colourless, no particles. Some peptides carry a faint tint — GHK-Cu is genuinely blue — but a cloudy solution means undissolved solids, not a bad batch.
9. **Label it.** Date, peptide, concentration in mcg/mL. Sharpie on the cap. In three weeks you will not remember which vial is which.
10. **Refrigerate at 2–8°C.** Never freeze a solution.

## The three mistakes that actually ruin a batch

**Spraying water directly onto the cake.** Foam, clumps, and a film of powder stuck to the shoulder of the vial that never dissolves. Your concentration is now lower than you calculated and you have no way to know by how much.

**Shaking.** Same outcome, faster. A vortex is not your friend here.

**Reconstituting with too little water.** A 5mg vial in 0.5mL is 10,000 mcg/mL, which means a 250 mcg draw is 2.5 units — a mark you cannot read reliably. You'll draw 3 and call it close enough. It isn't.

## When it won't dissolve

Give it twenty minutes of intermittent swirling at room temperature. A few peptides are slow. If there is still visible solid after that, the vial was probably warm during transit or the cake collapsed in storage. Do not add more water to force it; note the batch and tell the dealer.

If the solution turns cloudy after clearing, that's usually a pH problem with the water rather than the peptide. Swap water source and try a fresh vial.

## After it's mixed

Refrigerate, protect from light, use within four to six weeks. Draw with a fresh needle each time and swab the stopper every session — a reconstituted vial gets punctured dozens of times and each one is an entry point.

Do not freeze a reconstituted solution. Ice crystals shear the peptide and freeze-thaw cycling does it repeatedly.
