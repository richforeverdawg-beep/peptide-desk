These three get lumped together because all are injected, all are weekly, and all are bought for the same reason. They are not the same class of thing.

Short answer, before the detail:

- **Semaglutide** hits one receptor. Approved, licensed, cheapest to try, weakest of the three on weight in trials.
- **Tirzepatide** hits two. Approved, licensed, roughly double semaglutide's trial effect, and the cheapest per milligram of the three.
- **Retatrutide** hits three. **Not approved anywhere yet.** Largest trial effect by a wide margin and the most expensive per milligram.

If you want the one with the most evidence behind it, that's tirzepatide. If you want the one with the most effect in trials, that's retatrutide — and you accept that you're buying ahead of an approval.

## What each one actually binds to

This is the only structural difference that matters, and it is on the labels.

**Semaglutide** is a GLP-1 analogue with 94% sequence homology to human GLP-1, acting as a selective GLP-1 receptor agonist ([Wegovy prescribing information, FDA](https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/drugsatfda_docs/label/2026/215256s031lbl218316s003lbl.pdf)).

**Tirzepatide** is a GIP receptor and GLP-1 receptor agonist ([Zepbound prescribing information, FDA](https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/drugsatfda_docs/label/2026/217806s042lbl.pdf)).

**Retatrutide** is an agonist at the GIP, GLP-1 **and glucagon** receptors ([NEJM phase 2, Jastreboff et al.](https://www.nejm.org/doi/full/10.1056/NEJMoa2301972)). The glucagon arm is the reason it separates: glucagon receptor agonism adds energy expenditure on top of the appetite suppression the other two rely on.

One receptor, two, three. Everything downstream — effect size, side effect profile, price — follows from that.

## The approval gap is the biggest practical difference

Tirzepatide and semaglutide are licensed medicines. Retatrutide is not.

As of November 2026 retatrutide is not approved by the FDA for any use, and Lilly has said it intends to submit in Q1 2027 ([Drugs.com approval status](https://www.drugs.com/history/retatrutide.html), [Lilly](https://www.lilly.com/news/stories/what-to-know-about-retatrutide)).

That has a direct consequence for buyers. For tirzepatide and semaglutide there is a licensed label to compare your vial against — a licensed starting dose, titration schedule and maintenance dose. For retatrutide there is no approved dose at all. There are trial doses, and anyone quoting you a "retatrutide protocol" is quoting a trial arm, not a label. Treat those numbers accordingly.

Retatrutide is also priced as the premium product, which is the market agreeing with the trial data rather than the paperwork.

## What the trials showed — and how to read the numbers

Three landmark trials, and they are not directly comparable.

| Compound | Trial | Duration | Result | Population |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Semaglutide 2.4mg | [STEP-1](https://www.nejm.org/doi/abs/10.1056/NEJMoa2032183) | 68 weeks | −14.9% vs −2.4% placebo | Adults with overweight/obesity, no diabetes |
| Tirzepatide 15mg | [SURMOUNT-1](https://www.nejm.org/doi/full/10.1056/NEJMoa2206038) | 72 weeks | −20.9% (−15.0% at 5mg) | Adults with obesity/overweight, no diabetes |
| Retatrutide 12mg | [TRIUMPH-1](https://investor.lilly.com/news-releases/news-release-details/lillys-triple-agonist-retatrutide-delivered-powerful-weight-loss) | 80 weeks | −28.3% | Adults with obesity/overweight, no diabetes |

Read that table carefully and it stops being a leaderboard:

- **Different durations.** 68, 72 and 80 weeks. Weight loss curves are still falling at the end of all three, so longer trials flatter their compound.
- **Estimands differ.** The TRIUMPH-1 headline of 28.3% is the efficacy estimand — what happened in people who stayed on treatment. The same trial reported 25.0% under the treatment-regimen estimand in NEJM. Both are real; they answer different questions. Anyone quoting 28.3% without saying which estimand is selling.
- **Trial doses are not your dose.** Semaglutide's 2.4mg is a maintenance dose reached over months. Tirzepatide's 5mg is a starting dose. Retatrutide's 12mg is a maximum-tolerated dose in a trial. Comparing 15mg tirzepatide to 12mg retatrutide is fine; comparing 5mg to 12mg is not.
- **Nobody in these trials was injecting a research vial.** The participants got licensed product under supervision. Your result on a reconstituted vial is a reasonable expectation, not a guarantee.

## Cost per milligram

This is where the comparison gets uncomfortable, so here it is in the open. Bands are the ones published on this site, per vial, EUR, before shipping.

| Compound | Vial | Band | Per mg |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Semaglutide | 5mg | 32 € – 44 € | 6.40 € – 8.80 € |
| Tirzepatide | 10mg | 48 € – 65 € | 4.80 € – 6.50 € |
| Retatrutide | 10mg | 70 € – 95 € | 7.00 € – 9.50 € |

The counterintuitive result: **semaglutide costs more per milligram than tirzepatide.** The single-receptor, weakest-on-paper option is not the budget option. It is only cheaper per *vial* because the vial is half the size.

Per milligram is not the whole cost story either, because you don't consume milligrams — you consume doses. A 5mg weekly dose is 20mg a month, so two tirzepatide vials. A 2.4mg weekly semaglutide maintenance dose is under 10mg a month. Run your own numbers against the dose you intend; per-mg pricing punishes whoever is titrating slowly.

Retatrutide is the most expensive on both measures. That is not a mark against it — it's the newest molecule with the best trial data and no approval yet — but nobody should buy it thinking they're getting a bargain.

## Three comparisons people get wrong

**"Retatrutide is just stronger tirzepatide."** It isn't a stronger version of the same drug. The glucagon arm changes the mechanism, and with it the side effect profile. Expect the difference to be qualitative, not a multiplier.

**"Semaglutide is the safe starter because it's the mildest."** Mildest in trial effect. It is also the most expensive per milligram here, and being on a single agonist means less room before you're considering a switch anyway.

**"They're all interchangeable at equal milligrams."** 5mg of semaglutide is not 5mg of tirzepatide. Milligram equivalence across these three is meaningless without the receptor target attached.

## Which to pick

- **First order, most evidence, best price per mg:** tirzepatide.
- **Cheapest entry per vial, known name, willing to accept the weakest trial effect:** semaglutide.
- **Best trial effect, comfortable with an unapproved compound and premium pricing:** retatrutide.
